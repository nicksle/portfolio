Card 03: Redesigning Onboarding Experience

We explored multiple onboarding patterns and sequences to determine which approach would best balance user motivation, value demonstration, and required actions.

01 Six Tasks Shown Upfront
Overwhelming users before they experienced any value. Users saw a daunting checklist rather than an exciting journey.
02 Blocking Alerts Without Context
Users could navigate freely but hit generic blocking alerts without understanding why or what was required.
03 Disconnected Checklist Feel
Experience felt like isolated tasks rather than a purposeful journey toward meaningful interaction.
SLIDE 02 Exploring Different Onboarding Patterns
Image
pattern-comparison-matrix.svg
3x3 matrix comparing patterns across dimensions
↓ Annotations (Position: After Image)
01 Traditional Gated Flow
Users complete all setup steps before accessing features. Ensures data completeness but delays value demonstration and increases early drop-off risk.
02 Progressive Onboarding
Users complete essential steps first with optional tasks surfaced contextually. Balances setup with exploration but requires careful prioritization.
03 Late-Binding Onboarding
Users explore features freely with gentle nudges to complete profile. Fastest time-to-value but risks hitting friction points without necessary information.
SLIDE 03 Onboarding Card Presentation
Image
presentation-options-wireframes.svg
5 different card presentation approaches
↓ Annotations (Position: After Image)
01 Dismissible Carousel (Current)
Horizontal swipeable cards. Easy to dismiss and scroll past causing users to lose track of onboarding progress.
02 Sticky Progress Card
Persistent card at top of screen. Maintains awareness but could feel intrusive or block content if not carefully designed.
03 Progressive Checklist
Single card showing only current next step. Reduces overwhelm but hides full journey potentially creating uncertainty.
04 Expandable Summary Card
Collapsed state shows current task with expand option. Balances visibility with content space but adds interaction complexity.
05 Full-Screen Modal Flow
Each step takes over screen until complete. Ensures focus and completion but prevents exploration and feels restrictive.

01 Sequence A - Trust First
Build credibility before asking for engagement but delays value exposure. Good for risk-averse users.
02 Sequence B - Value First
Shows value immediately but creates frustration when users hit KYC gate without context.
03 Sequence C - Balanced Approach (Winner)
Quick survey primes expectations. Browse demonstrates value. KYC feels justified when users want to join. Highest completion rates in testing.
04 Sequence D - Social First
Leverages social motivation strongly but only works for referred users. Consider as alternate path.
05 Testing with 15 Participants
Measured: time to first interaction completion rates per step user confidence at decision points emotional response to blocking moments.
06 Key Finding: Balance Wins
Sequence C showed highest completion rates while maintaining user confidence. The survey + early browsing + contextual KYC created logical progression that users understood.
