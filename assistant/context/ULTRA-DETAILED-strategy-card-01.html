Card 01: Understanding Value & Building Trust

We conducted user testing to identify which value propositions resonated most with our target users and how we could effectively communicate trust and security throughout the onboarding experience.

Strategic Pillar: Build Trust Before Asking

Core Principle: Trust translates to action. Users won't share sensitive information or complete registration if they don't understand why it's needed or feel confident their data is secure.

The Problem We're Solving: Our research revealed that users abandoned the flow during KYC and profile setup because we were asking for sensitive information before establishing credibility. They had no context for why we needed their data, no assurance it would be protected, and no preview of what they'd gain by completing these steps.

Strategic Approach: We restructured the experience to build trust before making asks through: (1) Transparent Communication - explain the "why" before the "what", (2) Progressive Information Gathering - break forms into digestible steps, (3) Credibility Signals - display compliance badges and encryption indicators.

01 Asking Before Establishing Credibility
Users abandoned during KYC because we were asking for sensitive information (SSN, address, documents) before establishing credibility. They had no context for why we needed their data, creating immediate distrust.
💡 Strategic Context:
This was the primary finding from our UX session recordings. We observed users filling out initial fields, then exiting the moment they encountered SSN requests. Session replays showed hesitation patterns - users would pause, read the field multiple times, then close the app. This wasn't friction from complexity; it was friction from fear.
02 No Assurance of Data Protection
No visible security indicators, compliance badges, or privacy assurances at critical input moments left users feeling uncertain about data handling and platform legitimacy.
💡 Strategic Context:
User interviews revealed that people specifically looked for trust signals when asked for sensitive data. Participants mentioned expecting to see things like "bank-level encryption," "FDIC insured," or "secure connection" badges. The absence of these signals triggered immediate skepticism: "How do I know this is legitimate?" This insight directly informed our trust signal brainstorming in the next slides.

01 Quick Cash Access
"Access emergency funds when you need them" - Emphasizes immediate financial relief and speed. Focuses on the practical utility of getting money quickly without traditional barriers like credit checks or lengthy approval processes.
💡 Why This Matters:
This value prop directly addresses financial stress and urgency. It positions TANDA as a solution to immediate problems rather than long-term wealth building. During testing, this message showed the highest engagement across user segments, particularly resonating with users who had experienced unexpected expenses.
02 Community Savings
"Save together with friends and family" - Focuses on social connection and collective wealth building. Emphasizes the communal aspect of savings circles and the cultural tradition of ROSCAs (Rotating Savings and Credit Associations).
💡 Why This Matters:
This message taps into social motivation and cultural familiarity. For users with ROSCA experience, this immediately communicates "I understand what this is." However, testing showed this was too niche - users without cultural context didn't understand the benefit, and those who knew ROSCAs were sometimes skeptical of digital versions.
03 Financial Goals
"Achieve your financial goals faster" - Highlights specific outcomes like vacations, emergency funds, or down payments. Positions TANDA as a tool for reaching aspirational milestones rather than just accessing money.
💡 Why This Matters:
This framing appealed to goal-oriented users and created aspirational appeal. However, testing revealed it felt too distant for users in financial stress - they needed immediate help, not long-term planning. This message worked better as secondary messaging after users understood the core quick-access benefit.
04 No Credit Check
"Get money without credit checks" - Addresses financial inclusion and removes traditional barriers. Particularly relevant for users with poor credit history or those excluded from traditional financial systems.
💡 Why This Matters:
This message strongly resonated with a specific segment: users with credit concerns. However, it also raised questions about legitimacy ("If there's no credit check, how do they know I'll pay back?") and attracted some users looking to avoid credit consequences. We decided this works better as supporting copy rather than primary messaging.
05 Flexible Contributions
"Save at your own pace" - Emphasizes control and lack of rigid requirements. Communicates that TANDA adapts to user financial situations rather than demanding fixed commitments.
💡 Why This Matters:
This addressed anxiety around commitment and financial uncertainty. Users appreciated knowing they wouldn't be locked into payments they couldn't afford. However, this message tested as weak for acquisition - it reduced friction but didn't create motivation. It works better as reassurance copy during onboarding rather than primary value prop.
06 Social Accountability
"Stay motivated with your circle" - Leverages peer support and commitment. Positions the social aspect as a feature that helps users follow through on savings goals through friendly accountability.
💡 Why This Matters:
Testing showed mixed reactions. Some users loved the idea of social support; others felt pressure or embarrassment about financial struggles being visible to friends. This became a secondary benefit we highlight after users are already engaged, rather than leading with it during acquisition.
SLIDE 03 Trust Signal Brainstorm
🎯 Slide Purpose
Identify trust-building elements that would reduce anxiety around sensitive data sharing. These signals needed to be integrated at critical decision points - particularly during KYC when users were asked for SSN, address, and documents. The goal: make users feel confident and secure before asking for information.

↓ ANNOTATIONS (Position: After Image)
01 Encryption Indicators
Bank-level encryption badge with explanation of data security measures. Shows users that their information is protected with the same security standards as traditional financial institutions.
💡 Implementation Context:
User interviews revealed that "bank-level encryption" specifically resonated because it created parity with trusted institutions. Generic "secure" messaging didn't work - users needed concrete comparisons to institutions they already trusted. We tested different phrasings: "military-grade" felt overstated, "256-bit encryption" was too technical. "Bank-level" struck the right balance.
02 Compliance Badges
Display regulatory compliance (FDIC insured, SOC 2 certified) near sensitive input fields. Shows that TANDA meets official standards and is subject to regulatory oversight, not just self-proclaimed security.
💡 Implementation Context:
Card sorting exercises revealed users ranked official certifications higher than we expected. They wanted to know "Who's watching this company?" Displaying compliance badges answered that question without requiring users to dig through legal documents. We learned to place these at the moment of maximum anxiety (right before SSN input) rather than burying them in footer.
03 Privacy Policy Links
Clear, accessible privacy policy with plain language explanations. Provides transparency about data collection, usage, and storage without requiring users to parse legal jargon.
💡 Implementation Context:
Testing showed most users didn't read full privacy policies, but they wanted to know the option existed. The presence of a clear link signaled "we have nothing to hide." We experimented with progressive disclosure - offering a 3-sentence summary with "read full policy" option. The summary alone increased trust ratings without requiring full document reading.
04 Security Certifications
Third-party security certifications and audit results. Shows that independent experts have verified TANDA's security practices, not just internal claims.
💡 Implementation Context:
User research revealed skepticism toward self-reported security. Participants specifically mentioned wanting "proof from someone else." Third-party certifications provided that external validation. We tested different certifications and found SOC 2 and penetration testing results carried the most weight, even though most users didn't fully understand the technical details - the third-party validation itself was the trust signal.

↓ ANNOTATIONS (Position: After All Images)
01 Preference Tests with 25 Users
A/B tested value proposition messaging with 25 users in sign-up and onboarding screens. Measured engagement, conversion intent, and emotional response to different messages. "Quick Cash Access" showed highest engagement and conversion intent across all user segments.
💡 Testing Methodology:
We used preference testing with realistic mockups showing different value props in context. Users saw actual onboarding screens with varied messaging. We measured: (1) which messages users clicked first, (2) which they found most compelling in post-test interviews, (3) which generated strongest emotional response. "Quick Cash Access" dominated across all three metrics, showing 40% higher click-through than second-place message.
02 User Interviews About Trust Concerns
Conducted in-depth conversations with 12 users about trust concerns during sensitive data entry. Asked what would make them feel confident sharing SSN, address, and financial information. Encryption indicators and compliance badges were most frequently mentioned as confidence builders.
💡 Key Insights from Interviews:
Users specifically used phrases like "I'd want to see that lock icon" or "something that says it's official." When probed on what "official" meant, they described government oversight (FDIC) or industry standards (SOC 2). Interestingly, users didn't need to understand technical details - the presence of official-looking badges was sufficient. One participant said: "I don't know what SOC 2 means, but it looks like someone checked them out."
03 Finding: Emergency Funds as Primary Value Driver
"Access to emergency funds" resonated as the primary value driver across all user segments. Pairing this message with encryption indicators and transparent data usage statements at KYC entry increased user confidence by 34% and reduced abandonment during testing compared to control.
💡 Why This Combination Worked:
The value prop created motivation ("I need this"), while trust signals reduced anxiety ("I can trust this"). Testing different timing showed that introducing value BEFORE trust signals worked better than the reverse. Users needed to understand "what's in it for me" first, then trust signals validated their decision to proceed. Control group (generic messaging + no trust signals) showed 52% abandonment at SSN input; test group (emergency funds message + trust signals) showed only 18% abandonment.
04 Integration into Prototypes for Downstream Testing
We integrated validated value propositions and trust signals into wireframes and prototypes for sign-up and onboarding flows. This ensured that subsequent testing of flow patterns and interactions incorporated messaging that we already knew resonated, allowing us to focus testing efforts on UX patterns rather than re-validating messaging effectiveness.
💡 Strategic Decision:
By front-loading value prop and trust signal research, we created a solid foundation for all subsequent design work. When we tested onboarding sequences (Card 03), we didn't need to question whether our messaging worked - we already knew it did. This allowed us to isolate variables in later testing. For example, when testing Sequence A vs B vs C, we used the same validated messaging in all variants, so any performance differences could be attributed to sequence logic rather than messaging quality.
